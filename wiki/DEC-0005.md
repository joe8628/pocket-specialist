## Context
Providers carry mutually incompatible heavy dependencies: Surya v2 pins its own
`transformers` and spawns a Dockerized vLLM backend; PaddleOCR needs `paddlepaddle`;
UniMERNet needs its own model stack. Importing any of these into the main
ingestion process couples ordinary extraction to installs it should not require,
and forces irreconcilable version pins (e.g. Surya vs PP-DocLayout transformers).

## Decision
Such providers run **out-of-process** behind a uniform HTTP contract
(`POST /load`, `POST /detect` or `/extract`, `POST /offload`, `GET /health`),
each in its own isolated virtualenv under `services/<name>/`. The main runtime
talks to them over `PIPELINE_*_BASE_URL`. Provider imports happen only inside the
service runtime, never in the main extraction process.

## Alternatives considered
- In-process adapters for everything — rejected: irreconcilable dependency pins
  and makes plain extraction depend on UniMERNet/Paddle/Surya being installed.
- One mega-venv with all providers — rejected: version conflicts are unsolvable
  (Surya and PP-DocLayout disagree on `transformers`).

## Consequences
The repo-side `LayoutResult`/`FormulaResult` contracts stay stable even as
upstream objects change shape. Operational cost: services must be started and
warmed separately, and cold starts can exceed client timeouts (see DEC-0004 and
the Surya vLLM ~294s cold-start note). UniMERNet/Surya import-isolation is load-bearing.
Enforces the spec's "Provider isolation" principle. See CON-0002 for the contract.
